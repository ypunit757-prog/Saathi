# Photosynthesis (class 11 notes)

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It takes place inside chloroplasts, which contain the pigment chlorophyll that absorbs mostly red and blue light.

The process has two stages. The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes, where water is split and oxygen is released as a byproduct. These reactions also produce ATP and NADPH, which carry energy to the next stage.

The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma of the chloroplast and does not need light directly. It uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar, with the enzyme RuBisCO catalysing the first step of carbon fixation.

Several factors limit the rate of photosynthesis, including light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. At very high temperatures the enzymes involved begin to denature, so the rate falls sharply instead of continuing to rise.

C4 plants such as maize and sugarcane have an adaptation that concentrates carbon dioxide around RuBisCO. This reduces photorespiration and lets them photosynthesise efficiently in hot and bright conditions.
